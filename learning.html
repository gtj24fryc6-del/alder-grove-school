Our Learning

A curriculum that builds foundations, celebrates progress and prepares young people for life beyond Year 11.

What we want for every learner

Every pupil follows a learning pathway matched to how they learn best: an academic pathway (GCSE and Functional Skills), a vocational pathway (BTEC, Entry Level and applied learning), or a personalised pathway built around life skills and functional learning. Pathways are shaped by each pupil’s EHCP and can change as pupils progress.

Key Stage 3 focuses on rebuilding learning habits, closing gaps in reading and maths, and emotional regulation. Key Stage 4 focuses on qualifications, employability, independence and planning for what comes next.

We measure success from each pupil’s own starting point. Alongside academic progress, we focus on communication, independence, emotional regulation, friendships and resilience. Reading is assessed when pupils join and supported through phonics, guided reading and targeted intervention.

Learning overviews

Key Stage 3: English, maths and science; humanities, computing, PE, art, design and technology and modern foreign languages where appropriate; PSHE, citizenship and life skills; reading intervention; and therapeutic support across learning.

Key Stage 4: English, maths and science (GCSE, Functional Skills or applied); options including BTEC, Entry Level and vocational qualifications; careers and work-related learning; PSHE and RSE; and structured transition planning.

Tracking progress

Pupils are assessed when they join in literacy, numeracy, communication and regulation needs. Teachers assess progress daily, and formal assessments take place three times a year, tracked on Arbor against each pupil’s EHCP targets. Reading is tracked using standardised assessments. Families receive written reports, termly parents’ evenings and an annual EHCP review.

PSHE, RSE & wellbeing

PSHE is taught through the Jigsaw scheme, alongside dedicated Relationships and Sex Education and Health Education blocks. Teaching is adapted to each pupil’s stage of development and delivered in a structured, trauma-informed way. Families can ask to see sample materials. Parents may request withdrawal from sex education only, not from Relationships Education, Health Education or science, by writing to the Headteacher.

Enrichment & wider learning

Educational visits, community learning, outdoor education, physical development, creative and cultural experiences, and therapeutic enrichment where needed form part of the wider curriculum. Pupils also learn about caring for the environment through outdoor learning. Visits are risk-assessed for participating pupils, with staffing set by need. Everyday learning outside the classroom is agreed when a pupil starts; longer or higher-risk visits need specific consent in advance.

Next steps & careers

Careers Leader: Sakriye Moallim. The careers programme runs through Years 7 to 11, follows the Gatsby Benchmarks and is adapted to each pupil’s pathway. Pupils have work-related activities in Years 7 to 9 and a week of work experience in Years 10 and 11, planned flexibly around their needs. Transition planning is part of EHCP reviews from Year 9. Pupils learn about further education, apprenticeships, technical education, supported internships and employment routes.
